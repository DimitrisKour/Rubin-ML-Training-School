A three-day LINCC Frameworks training school on transient classification, anomaly detection, and similarity search, taught on ZTF alerts and carried over to Rubin Data Preview 2.

About the school

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory alert stream has been public since February 2026, and the catalogs of Data Preview 2 (DP2), the first release built from LSST Camera images, became available to data-rights holders through the Rubin Science Platform in July 2026. Turning these data into classifications and discoveries requires machine-learning workflows that most early-career researchers have not yet had the chance to build on real survey data.

This school trains graduate students, postdocs, and early-career researchers to classify transients, detect anomalies, and search for similar events in alert data, using two tools: AppleCiDEr, the multimodal transient classifier and anomaly-detection pipeline developed through the LINCC Incubator, and Hyrax, the LINCC Frameworks framework for training and analyzing machine-learning models on survey data. Participants build each workflow on labeled Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) alerts during the first two days, deploy models inside the BOOM alert broker, and transfer their workflows to Rubin DP2 during a hackathon on the third day.

The school is held in person at the University of Minnesota for about 20 participants. Lunches and breaks are provided, and limited travel support is available, with priority for students and for researchers from smaller and minority-serving institutions. It is supported by the LSST Discovery Alliance through the LINCC Frameworks Community Events program.

What participants will build

Classifiers on alert data

Train supervised classifiers for transients and variable stars on labeled ZTF alerts with AppleCiDEr, and train a light-curve model in Hyrax.

Anomaly detection and similarity search

Score unusual events with AppleCiDEr and LUNA, and find analogs of a source through nearest-neighbor search in embedding space.

Models running in a broker

Export models to ONNX, run them inside BOOM, and expose them to agents and SkyPortal through MCP servers.

Workflows transferred to Rubin DP2

Port the ZTF workflows to DP2 light curves on the Rubin Science Platform during the hackathon, accounting for the change in bands, units, and cadence.

Open tutorial notebooks

Every session ships as an openly licensed notebook in the school repository, and hackathon projects are added there as reusable examples.

Seeds for science proposals

Leave with a working project on Rubin data that can grow into a proposal or a paper.

Participants and preparation

The school is designed for graduate students, postdocs, and early-career researchers who are comfortable with Python and Jupyter notebooks. Prior experience with machine learning or with Rubin data is helpful but not required, and members of the Transient and Variable Stars Science Collaboration and new Rubin users are especially welcome.

Registration
Selected participants receive logistics and preparation material by email. Questions about registration go to asasli@umn.edu.
Travel support
Limited support for travel and lodging is available, with priority for students and for researchers from smaller and minority-serving institutions.

Before the school

Please complete the three steps below before Monday, November 9. Account problems can take several days to resolve, so we recommend starting as soon as possible and writing to asasli@umn.edu if any step fails.

  1. BOOM alert streams through Babamul. Create an account on the Babamul deployment of BOOM at babamul.caltech.edu, then open your profile page, where you will find a Kafka username and password for reading the ZTF and Rubin alert streams and an API token for cross-matches, photometry, and cone searches. Keep these three values at hand: in the Day 1 activity you copy them into a .env file next to the notebook. A backup deployment runs at babamul.umn.edu, with its own credentials on its own profile page.
  2. Rubin Science Platform. Log in at data.lsst.cloud with your Rubin data-rights account and start a session in the Notebooks aspect once, which confirms that your account works and creates your home directory. The Day 3 hackathon runs on DP2 catalogs there. Participants without Rubin data rights work on ZTF data or on live Rubin alerts through Babamul during the hackathon.
  3. Software and computing. A pre-configured environment and the tutorial notebooks are distributed through the school repository at github.com/asasli/Rubin-ML-Training-School, with compute provided through the Minnesota Supercomputing Institute. Bring a laptop with a recent web browser and a terminal.

Code

  • AppleCiDEr Multimodal transient classifier and anomaly-detection pipeline developed through the LINCC Incubator
  • FLARE Photometric classification and anomaly-detection module of AppleCiDEr, running in production on Fritz
  • LUNA Classifier-agnostic library for novelty and anomaly detection on model embeddings and logits
  • BOOM Multi-survey alert broker for ZTF and Rubin, written in Rust
  • Babamul Python client for the public Babamul alert streams built on BOOM
  • SkyPortal Open-source platform for discovering, following up, and characterizing transients
  • Hyrax LINCC Frameworks framework for training and analyzing machine-learning models on survey data
